Public flight observations

Open /#flights or Situation room > Flights. GET /v1/flights uses the existing authentication middleware.

Sources. Every five minutes the worker scans two community ADS-B networks in parallel, ADSB.lol
(https://api.adsb.lol/docs, ODbL 1.0) and adsb.fi open data (https://adsb.fi, non-commercial use),
using sixteen 250-nautical-mile query circles each, eastern circles first (ADSB.lol paced at
8 s per request after it rate-limited at 4 s; adsb.fi at 2 s). Their receiver sets
differ, so the merged picture sees more aircraft than either alone. One extra request per scan reads
ADSB.lol's military list (/v2/mil). Each network has its own pacing, daily request cap and back-off:
a 429/503 pauses only that network (Retry-After, default 10 minutes). airplanes.live was evaluated
and refuses unregistered API clients (HTTP 403).

Coverage. Community networks have very few receivers in central and eastern Africa. On 2026-10-19
at ~01:00 Kinshasa time both networks showed zero aircraft over Kinshasa, Goma, Nairobi, Entebbe,
Kigali, Lusaka, Ndola and Luanda; the first test scan found four aircraft, all on the approaches.
Most traffic over the DRC is therefore not visible, and an empty map does not mean empty airspace.
Positions are never extrapolated; positions older than ten minutes disappear, older than two are
marked stale. Two ways to improve coverage: feed receivers in the DRC (an RTL-SDR and Raspberry Pi
in Goma, Kinshasa or Lubumbashi, feeding ADSB.lol/adsb.fi) or add a paid provider with satellite
ADS-B (for example Flightradar24 API or FlightAware AeroAPI).

Region. Aircraft inside the approximate national polygon are region "drc"; aircraft within the
DRC bounding box widened by about 2° (Kigali, Entebbe, Brazzaville, Ndola...) are "approach" and
shown dimmed, with a toggle to hide them. Not an official FIR boundary.

Military and privacy. Military aircraft that broadcast ADS-B are shown and labelled (database
military flag, or a military/state operator name). Aircraft whose owners enrolled them in the PIA
or LADD privacy programmes are excluded.

Classification (each aircraft states its basis; none establishes payload, passengers or mission):
  military      ADS-B database military flag, or operator name of an air force/army/state air arm
  humanitarian  UNO/UNH (United Nations) callsign, or UN/WFP/UNHAS/MONUSCO/ICRC/MAF/ASF operator
  cargo         adsbdb airframe registered as a freighter variant (e.g. "747 48EF", "777 F6N",
                BCF/BDSF/P2F), an operator of dedicated freighters (e.g. Astral Aviation ACP,
                Cargolux CLX), a cargo/freight/logistics operator name, or an AN-12/26/32/72/74,
                IL-76, AN-124 or L-382 airframe
  airline       callsign prefix matches the public airline directory
  unknown       nothing matched
Helicopters, light aircraft and heavies come from the ADS-B emitter category; squawk 7500/7600/7700
and emergency status are flagged.

Destination. adsbdb (https://www.adsbdb.com) supplies the published route for a callsign (origin
and destination airports with coordinates), the aircraft record (type, registration, registered
owner) and the airline. A route is marked plausible only when the observed position lies near the
origin-destination path (detour within 30% + 300 km); otherwise it is shown as unverified, because
callsigns are reused. Lookups are cached in SQLite (aircraft 30 days, routes 12 hours, airlines
7 days, misses one day) and limited to 40 new aircraft per scan.

Settings. FLIGHTS_ENABLED=0 disables collection. FLIGHTS_POLL_SECONDS defaults to 300 (minimum 300).
FLIGHTS_DAILY_REQUEST_LIMIT defaults to 5000 per network per UTC day (ADSB.lol, adsb.fi and adsbdb
counted separately); 0 stops requests. This counts requests, not dollars; no paid key or AI call is
used. Only the latest snapshot is stored; no positional history is retained.
